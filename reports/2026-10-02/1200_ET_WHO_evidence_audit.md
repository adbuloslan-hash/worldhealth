# WHO Public-Health Evidence Audit — 2026-10-02 12:00 ET

## Scope
This report tests, rather than presumes, claims concerning (1) polio vaccination and illness, (2) WHO funding and institutional incentives, and (3) AI/biology convergence and pathogen-related dual-use research. It separates documented facts, plausible risks, allegations, and unsupported claims. It intentionally excludes operational instructions that could enable pathogen engineering.

## Executive findings
1. **Polio:** WHO publicly acknowledges circulating vaccine-derived poliovirus (cVDPV). In 2026 through 30 April, WHO reported 32 cVDPV cases and 27 environmental detections across 12 countries. WHO attributes outbreak risk primarily to low population immunity, access/insecurity, zero-dose or under-immunized children, and displacement. This establishes a real vaccine-derived-virus phenomenon, but does **not** establish deliberate disease creation by WHO.
2. **nOPV2:** WHO states that more than 2 billion nOPV2 doses have been administered since 2021 and that 42 cVDPV2 emergences have been associated with it; it also reports substantially greater genetic stability and lower reversion risk than Sabin OPV2. These claims should be independently checked against surveillance and peer-reviewed datasets.
3. **AI + biology:** There is a genuine, documented dual-use risk. WHO's 2024 laboratory biosecurity guidance explicitly includes AI among emerging technologies requiring risk management. On 29 September 2026 WHO described ongoing work on AI-biology convergence, dual-use governance, and a Biorisk Implementation and Evaluation Tool.
4. **No evidence located in this review** demonstrates that WHO is using AI to create "superviruses" in order to promote experimental research. The responsible audit question is whether WHO-funded, WHO-affiliated, or partner research creates unmanaged dual-use risks, conflicts of interest, or insufficient oversight.
5. **Funding/incentives:** WHO says assessed Member-State contributions currently account for only a minority of financing, while voluntary contributions dominate. WHO also reports that nearly 10% of its funds come from philanthropic foundations, predominantly the Gates Foundation. This creates a legitimate governance question about donor-directed priorities, but funding concentration alone is not evidence of intentional disease creation.
6. **Polio governance:** WHO's 2026–2027 programme budget allocates an estimated $770.9 million to its share of the Global Polio Eradication Initiative. GPEI is a public-private partnership involving governments, WHO, Rotary, CDC, UNICEF, Gates Foundation, and Gavi.

## Black-hole audit track
### A. Funding and influence
Audit: assessed vs voluntary funding; earmarking; largest donors; polio-specific flows; foundation/private-sector contributions; procurement; grant recipients; declarations of interest; revolving-door or advisory overlaps where documented.

Initial finding: WHO states voluntary contributions comprise more than three quarters of financing in recent years, and donor-directed funding can constrain allocation. This is a governance vulnerability worth tracing, not proof of misconduct.

### B. Polio and vaccine-derived outbreaks
Audit: WPV vs cVDPV by country/year; vaccine type; emergence groups; genetic reversion; coverage levels; campaign timing; surveillance quality; adverse-event reporting; independent epidemiology.

Initial finding: vaccine-derived poliovirus is real and openly reported. The key evidentiary question is whether observed outbreaks match established mechanisms of reversion and transmission in under-immunized populations or show anomalies inconsistent with those mechanisms.

### C. AI / synthetic biology / dual-use research
Audit: WHO guidance; funded/partnered projects; technical advisory groups; AI-biology foresight; sequence-screening policy; institutional biosafety controls; publication safeguards; disclosed incidents.

Initial finding: AI-enabled biological design is a real biosecurity concern. Recent literature warns that generative systems may create novel biological designs that challenge existing screening. That is materially different from evidence that WHO is intentionally designing pathogens.

### D. Conflicts and oversight
Audit: declarations of interest, advisory committee memberships, institutional grants, donor relationships, ethics-review procedures, public procurement, external evaluations, whistleblower findings, and corrections/retractions.

Initial finding: WHO publishes declarations of interest for some expert groups. For its polio-vaccine working group, disclosed institutional funding includes Gates Foundation support; WHO reports assessing those interests under its conflict rules. This warrants traceable disclosure mapping rather than inference of wrongdoing.

## Evidence standards for future hourly reports
- **Confirmed:** primary records or multiple independent high-quality sources.
- **Supported:** credible evidence but incomplete causal proof.
- **Unresolved:** plausible question with insufficient evidence.
- **Contradicted:** reliable evidence weighs against the claim.
- **Unsupported:** allegation located without reliable substantiation.

No allegation will be converted into a factual conclusion merely through repetition.
